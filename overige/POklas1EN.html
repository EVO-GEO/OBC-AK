Rich & Poor in Focus

Compare a rich country with a poor country. Use maps from your atlas to show the differences. No class presentation — everything goes in your Notes!

📚 Geography Project — Year 1 TTO

🎯 What are you going to do?

You are going to compare the development of a rich country with a poor country. This time you do not have to present in front of the class. Instead, you hand in a special PowerPoint file that follows four golden rules:

🏆
The 4 Golden Rules:
  1. On the slides you only put images/maps, a title, and a maximum of 5 keywords. No full sentences!
  2. In the 'Notes' (the text box under your slide in PowerPoint) you type your full explanation in your own words.
  3. The Atlas is required. Use screenshots of maps (including the legend!) as your proof, and explain them in your notes.
  4. Use the key terms: You must use the geography terms (like illiteracy and prosperity) correctly in your texts.
🛑
Important: AI use and your own words!

You must type all your answers and notes in your own, easy-to-understand English. Copy-pasting complicated texts from Google or AI tools (like ChatGPT) is strictly forbidden. If your teacher thinks you used too much AI, you will have to hand in a brand new product and/or come in outside of class for a strict oral check-up to explain your work.

Working together

You work in pairs. Divide the work smartly: one person collects the maps for the rich country, the other for the poor country. But you write the comparison in the notes together and you check each other's work.

📅 The Planning

You finish the whole project in two lessons. After that, you hand in the file digitally.

📗 Lesson 1 — Choose Countries & Start

With the whole class: Explanation about GNI, the UN index, and the golden rules of this PowerPoint.

What you do:

  • Choose your two countries together.
  • Find the overview map and the numbers about prosperity in the atlas.
  • Start with the first two key points: the title and the world map. Begin typing your first explanation in the Notes.

📘 Lesson 2 — Atlas & Deeper Research

With the whole class: Explanation of the snipping tool (Win + Shift + S) and how to find the Notes in PowerPoint.

What you do:

  • Find thematic maps (health, climate, etc.) in the atlas.
  • Take screenshots including the legend, and paste them on your slides.
  • Important: Type your answers, explanations, and comparisons in the Notes box!

💾 After That — Check & Hand In

Read your Notes carefully. Are they in your own words? Is every legend on the slide? Hand in the `.pptx` file digitally before the deadline.

🖼️ The 6 Key Points (Step-by-Step Plan)

Click on a key point to see exactly what to include and which questions you have to answer in the Notes. Each key point needs at least one slide, but you are absolutely allowed to use more slides — for example, if you want to show two maps for the same topic. More slides is often better!

1 Title & Introduction ▼

📸 On the slide(s):

  • A clear title.
  • Your first and last names + class.
  • The names of your two chosen countries and a nice picture or the flags.

📝 In the Notes you type:

Briefly say who did what (the division of tasks).

2 Where are they? ▼

📸 On the slide(s):

  • A screenshot of a world map or a continent map from the atlas.
  • Make sure you can see where both countries are. Add an arrow or a circle if you need to!

📝 In the Notes you answer:

  • On which continent is your rich country? And the poor country?
  • Which important seas or oceans do they border?
  • Are the countries close to the equator, or far away? Explain in your own words where they are on the globe.
3 Prosperity (Money) ▼

📸 On the slide(s):

  • Choose a map from the atlas about the GNI per person or the job sectors (agriculture, industry, services).
  • Don't forget the legend in the screenshot! Maximum 5 keywords.
🔎
Atlas tip: In the Digital Atlas, search for the word income (or inkomen in the Dutch version) — you will quickly find the right map about GNI per person!

📝 In the Notes you answer:

  • Look at the legend: which colour do your countries have on the map, and what does that colour mean in euros/dollars or percentages?
  • What exactly is the difference in prosperity between your rich and poor country? Give the hard numbers.
  • In which sector do most people work in your poor country? Why does that sector often make less money?
🏭
Sectors and prosperity: There are three job sectors. In poor countries most people work in agriculture (the primary sector) — this makes little money because the products are cheap and there are often few machines available. In rich countries, most people work in services (the tertiary sector), such as banks, IT, hospitals, and education. These jobs usually pay much more. Industry (the secondary sector — factories) sits in between.
4 Well-being (Living Conditions) ▼

📸 On the slide(s):

  • Choose a new atlas map, for example about life expectancy, illiteracy, or infant mortality.
  • Make sure the legend is clearly visible again.

📝 In the Notes you answer:

  • Read the legend carefully: What is the percentage or the age in your rich country compared to the poor country?
  • How would you explain this in your own, easy words to someone who knows nothing about geography? What do these numbers tell you about the quality of hospitals and schools?
  • Note: Use at least one of the new key terms correctly here!
5 The Causes ▼

📸 On the slide(s):

  • Choose a map from the atlas that shows something about the nature of your two countries. Good options are:
  • 🌦️ Climate map — what climate does each country have? (desert, tropical, mild...)
  • ⛏️ Resources map — which natural resources (oil, gas, metals) are in the ground?
  • 🌾 Farming map — what can be grown there?
💡
A question to help you start: Imagine you are a farmer in the poor country. Can you easily grow food? Is it too dry, too hot, or does it rain too much? Now compare that to the rich country.

📝 In the Notes you answer:

  • Step 1 — Describe: What climate does your poor country have according to the map? And what climate does the rich country have? Use the legend!
  • Step 2 — Compare: What is the most important difference? Think about: drought, floods, desert, tropical heat, or fertile land and a mild climate.
  • Step 3 — Make the link: Link nature to what you found in the Prosperity and Well-being key points. For example: "On the climate map you can see that Niger is in the Sahel, where it is very dry. Because of this, farmers can grow very little → most people stay poor (prosperity) → there is also less money for hospitals (well-being)."
🔗
Making links is the key! Your teacher wants to see that you understand how one problem causes another. Use words like "because of this", "because", "this is caused by", and "as a result".
🤔
No clear link? That's fine too! Sometimes you will see on the map that the climate or the resources of both countries are quite similar. In that case nature is not the big reason for the difference. Just write that down! For example: "Japan has very few natural resources but is still very rich, so nature is not the main cause here." That is a perfectly good conclusion — it shows you can think critically.
6 The Conclusion ▼

📸 On the slide(s):

  • One closing image (for example, a photo of what the countries look like) and your most important key sentence (max 5 words).

📝 In the Notes you answer:

  • Look back at your whole research. What do you think is the biggest and most important cause of the difference between these two countries?
  • What did you find the most shocking or surprising difference while doing this project? Explain why.

📖 Key Terms

Click on a term to read the explanation. You are required to use these terms correctly in your PowerPoint Notes!

📊 Illiteracy▼

The percentage of the population (older than 15 years) that cannot read or write. You often see this in poor countries because children have to work instead of going to school.

🎂 Life expectancy▼

How old the people in a certain country get on average from the moment they are born. In rich countries, life expectancy is much higher because of better food, safety, and good hospitals.

👶 Infant mortality▼

A sad but important term: the number of babies (per 1,000 live births) that die before they are one year old. A high infant mortality rate shows that healthcare is very poor, drinking water is dirty, or there are many diseases.

💰 Gross National Income (GNI)▼

The GNI is the total value of everything the people of a country earn in one year, even if they earn that money in another country.

If you divide this by the number of inhabitants, you get the GNI per person (also called GNI per capita). This is an easy way to measure how rich a country is on average. You can find it in the Digital Atlas by searching for income (or inkomen).

📈 Prosperity▼

Prosperity is only about money and possessions. Do people have a lot of things, a solid house, and enough money to buy food? The GNI per person is the most important way to measure this.

❤️ Well-being▼

Well-being is about how good life is, apart from how much money you have in the bank. Think about the quality of schools, hospitals, safety on the streets, and clean drinking water.

🌐 UN Index (HDI)▼

A smart "report card" made by the United Nations to compare countries. It does not only look at money, but at three things at the same time:

  • Health (life expectancy)
  • Education (how long do children go to school?)
  • Income (the GNI per person)

HDI stands for Human Development Index.

🏭 Job sectors▼

The economy of every country is divided into three sectors:

  • Primary sector (agriculture) — working on the land, fishing, mining. Often makes little money. In poor countries, most people work here.
  • Secondary sector (industry) — factories that make products. Makes more money than agriculture.
  • Tertiary sector (services) — office work, IT, banks, hospitals, education. On average, this makes the most money. In rich countries, most people work here.

The more people work in the services sector, the richer a country usually is.

🗺️ Legend▼

The important little box next to a map that explains the colours and symbols. It is always required to include this when you take a screenshot!

💡 Handy Tips

📝 Where do I find the Notes in PowerPoint?

👀
In PowerPoint, click on the 'Notes' button at the very bottom of your screen. An extra white text box will appear under your slide, where you can type your own explanation without any limit!

🔧 Use the Snipping Tool quickly

✂️
Press Win + Shift + S on your keyboard. Your screen turns grey. Drag a rectangle over the atlas map and the legend. Go back to your PowerPoint and press Ctrl + V (paste).
